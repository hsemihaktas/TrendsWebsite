```text
Design a [pottery brand/artisan product/meditation app/Japanese restaurant] using Wabi-Sabi aesthetic principles.

Wabi-Sabi is the Japanese philosophy of finding beauty in imperfection, impermanence, and incompleteness. It is the exact opposite of Western design's quest for polish and perfection — and it is extraordinarily calming as a visual experience.

The visual language: asymmetry instead of balance (but not random — purposeful asymmetry, as in a ceramic bowl that is slightly off-center), organic textures that show age or use (the patina on a brass surface, the rough edge of handmade paper), natural materials in their unprocessed state (unfinished wood grain, raw clay).

Color palette: all earth tones, all desaturated. Warm stone, pale sand, aged bone, moss gray, weathered copper. No pure white, no pure black — everything has a slight warmth or age to it.

Typography: a serif with personality — not Garamond's precision, but something slightly irregular, as if letterpress-printed. Wide spacing, lowercase preferred.

Space: generous, deliberate negative space. Wabi-Sabi resists crowding. A single ceramic bowl in the center of a large expanse of cream space IS the design.

Apply this to my [brand/product]: [describe what you're creating — it should connect to craft, nature, slow living, or contemplative practices]. The result should make the viewer slow down — feel the texture, appreciate the silence around the objects.
```
